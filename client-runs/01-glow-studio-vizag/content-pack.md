## 1. The strategy in four lines

1. **Lead with recognition, not with the brand.** Every hook opens on something the customer already feels — 40 minutes with a dryer, the fear of damage, ₹12,000 already spent.
2. **Show the process, not the promise.** The strand check, the patch test and the written price are what separate you from every other studio in the city.
3. **Say one honest thing per ad.** "You'll be in the chair three to four hours." "We'll say no if your hair can't take it." Trust is built by what you're willing to admit.
4. **Ask for something small.** A photo on WhatsApp, not a booking. The first step must cost the customer nothing.

## 3. The six scripts

### SCRIPT 1 — "40 minutes every morning" (PAS · 30s)
**Say:** "If your hair takes 40 minutes to dry, this is for you. That's 40 minutes every morning. Two hundred and forty hours a year — standing there with a dryer, and it's frizzy again by lunch. There's a reason it doesn't hold. Thick hair in coastal humidity needs a protein treatment, not more heat. It takes three hours here — once. Three to five months of manageable hair. And if your hair isn't right for it, they'll tell you before you pay. Send one photo on WhatsApp — you'll get a price range in ten minutes."
**Shoot:** bathroom mirror with the dryer → studio: strand check, wash, treatment applied, seal, aftercare sheet → phone showing the reply.
**Text on screen:** `40 minutes. Every morning.` → `240 hours a year` → `3–5 months` → `Photo → price in 10 min`

### SCRIPT 2 — "Same hair. Three hours apart." (Before/After · 25s)
**Say:** "Same hair. Three hours apart. This is how it came in — frizzy since the last monsoon, hadn't been trimmed in a year. Three hours. Strand check, clarifying wash, protein treatment section by section, then a seal. This is month four, not day one. That's the part most places don't tell you. Send a photo — get a price in ten minutes. No appointment needed to ask."
**Shoot:** split-screen before/after → hands through dry ends → each treatment step (2s each, hands only) → recent result footage → WhatsApp end card.
**Text on screen:** `Same hair` → `3 hours` → `Month 4 — not day 1` → `Results vary`

### SCRIPT 3 — "₹12,000 and two salons" (Storytime · 35s)
**Say:** "I spent twelve thousand rupees on keratin before I found this place. Two other salons. The first one, it lasted three weeks. The second one, my ends went like straw. I honestly thought keratin just wasn't for my hair. The difference here was the first fifteen minutes. She checked my hair section by section and said the last treatment had been done too fast, and that's why it didn't hold. Fair warning — you'll be in the chair three to four hours. Bring a charger. But it's been five months and I still haven't touched the iron. Send a photo of your hair on WhatsApp. They'll tell you honestly if it'll work."
**Shoot:** client in the chair (or back-of-head + voice) → old photos on a phone → stylist checking strands → finished hair in window light.
**Text on screen:** `₹12,000. Two salons.` → `Lasted 3 weeks` → `3–4 hours. Bring a charger.` → `Honest answer, free`

### SCRIPT 4 — "Will keratin damage my hair?" (Objection-crusher · 28s)
**Say:** "Keratin didn't ruin your hair. The shortcut did. The question we get every single day: 'Will keratin damage my hair?' Fair question. Here's the honest answer. We don't skip them — and we do a patch test 24 hours before every colour service. And if your hair genuinely can't take it, we say no. We'll suggest a repair plan instead. Ask us anything on WhatsApp before you book. Free, and no pressure."
**Shoot:** you, waist-up, to camera, near the window. No music for the first 20 seconds. Minimal cuts.
**Text on screen:** `"Will keratin damage my hair?"` → `Clarifying wash` → `The seal` → `Patch test · 24 hrs` → `We'll say no if it's wrong`

### SCRIPT 5 — "My friend said don't go" (Review-reaction · 22s)
**Say:** "My friend told me not to come here. She was wrong. This is the review she left after her keratin. Read the last line. That's the part I didn't believe.
