## 3. What to say to the person on camera

**The 4-line directing script (read this to Sanjana):**
> "Don't memorise it. Say it like you'd tell a friend at a tea stall — if you mess up a word, just keep going, I'll cut it. Talk to the phone like it's one person, not an audience. And pause for a second before you start."

**Three prompts to use when she freezes:**
1. "Just tell me the story of the girl who bleached her hair twice. Forget the script."
2. "Say the first sentence again — the rest doesn't matter."
3. "Talk to me, not the camera. Look at me and say it."

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't let her read off a screen at eye level — she'll go flat. Print it, tape it beside the camera.
- No scripted smile at the start. No "hi guys".
- Don't change her voice for the CTA — it's the biggest authenticity tell.
- Don't retake more than three times. Take three, move on, pick the one with the stumble — that's usually the best one.

## 4. Phone settings & quick tech sheet

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Orientation | Vertical (9:16) — hold the phone upright |
| Resolution | 1080p at 30fps (4K only if you have storage and a good laptop) |
| Exposure & focus | Tap and **lock** on the subject's face before recording |
| Lens | Wipe it. Every time. This is 80% of "why does my video look bad" |
| Light | Stand with the window **in front of** the subject, never behind. 11 AM–1 PM is best in this studio |
| Holding the phone | Elbows tucked in, phone close to your chest, breathe out before you press record |
| Audio | Speaker 30–45 cm from the phone. Turn off the fan and the AC. Record the CTA line separately if the shop is noisy — then lay it over |

**If you have ₹500 to spend:** a ₹300 phone tripod and a ₹200 clip-on mic will improve the output more than any other purchase. Both are optional.

1. Studio signboard from the street (shows the bakery landmark below)
2. Hand wiping down the chair
3. Hair falling through fingers, backlit by the window
4. Product bottles arranged on the counter
5. The studio clock
6. Steam or towel texture close-up
7. Door opening, client entering (backs only)
8. Tea being handed to a waiting client
9. Comb, scissors and clips laid out on the trolley
10. The 4 chairs, wide, morning light
11. Fish-tank / plant / any small detail that makes the space feel real
12. Sanjana laughing at something off-camera

> 12 clips × 3–5 seconds = about 50 seconds of cutaway material, enough to build a month of ads without making anyone look at the camera again.

## 6. Consent & permission

**Say this before filming a customer:**
> "Would you mind if I filmed a short clip of your hair for our Instagram? Your face doesn't have to be in it — most people just show the back. Totally fine if you'd rather not."

**Send this on WhatsApp immediately, and screenshot the reply:**
> "Hi [name], just confirming you're okay with us using today's video/photo on our Instagram and in our ads. You can ask us to remove it any time. Reply 'yes, okay' if that's fine. Thank you! — Glow Studio"

**If they say no — the fallback ladder:**
1. Back of head / hair only (perfect for keratin before-and-after)
2. Hands only (works for facials, nails, products)
3. A staff member plays the "customer" in the scene
4. Product and b-roll only, with a real review read as voiceover

Every script has at least one of these four fallbacks, so a "no" never stops the shoot.

## 7. Edit handoff

**Folder structure:**
```
Glow-Studio-Ads/
├── 01-raw/            (everything as filmed, keep originals)
├── 02-audio/          (voiceovers, clean CTA lines)
├── 03-broll/          (the 12 clips, named by number: b01-signboard.mp4)
├── 04-exports/        (final files, named: glow-script1-reels-30s-v1.mp4)
└── 05-photos/         (stills for thumbnails and Google Business)
```

**Naming convention:** `glow-script<N>-<platform>-<duration>-v<version>.mp4`
Example: `glow-script2-meta-15s-v2.mp4`

**What to keep for repurposing:** every shoot should yield at least **3 hook variants** (swap only the first 2 seconds) and **2 CTA variants** (swap only the last 3). That's 6 ads from one shoot.

## 8. Cost & time reality

| Item | Time / cost |
|---|---|
| Filming (3 sessions) | ~4 hours, spread over 3 days, during working hours |
| Editing in CapCut | 30–45 min per script, 3 cutdowns each |
| Optional clip-on mic | ₹200–₹500 |
| Optional phone tripod | ₹300–₹800 |
| **Total cash cost** | **₹0–₹1,300** |
| **Total staff time** | **~6 hours** |
| **Output** | 6 scripts × 3 cutdowns = up to 18 ads |

**What you can skip if the budget is zero:** the tripod (prop the phone on the desk), the mic (film between 11 AM and 1 PM when the studio is quietest), and Script 3 (needs a consenting client).
