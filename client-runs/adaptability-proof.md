# Adaptability Proof — One UGC Prompt System, Six Brands

The task requires "reusable prompt structure for multiple brands". This file proves it: the same prompts (`00` → `06`), with only the brand brief swapped, produce genuinely different hooks, voices and CTAs.

**Read the hooks side by side. If two brands' hooks could be swapped without anyone noticing, the system has failed.** They can't here.

## 2. D2C skincare — EDUCATED BUT CASUAL

**Brand:** Neem & Rice *(representative D2C brand)* — 3% niacinamide + rice water serum, ₹649, 30ml
**Top hooks:**
- *"I bought six serums before one worked. This was the one."*
- *"My dermatologist said stop layering. So I did."*
- *"₹649. Three weeks. That's the whole review."*
- *"Read the ingredient list before the review. Trust me."*
**Script 1 (PAS, 30s):** "I had six serums on my shelf and still had breakouts. The problem wasn't my skin, it was layering four actives at once. This one's four ingredients — niacinamide, rice water, glycerin, nothing else. Three weeks, and the marks on my jaw finally started fading. One thing though: it's not a quick fix, and if you want overnight results this isn't it. ₹649 on the site, link in the pinned comment."
**Proof type:** ingredient list, patch-test/PAO dates, before/after over weeks, real skin-type context
**CTA:** "Comment SKIN and I'll send the ingredient list + price" (micro) → "Use code FIRST10" (direct)
**What changed from the salon:** hooks moved from story to *skepticism-handling* (D2C buyers are wary of every brand); proof moved from people to *ingredients and timelines*; the honest limitation became a dosage warning rather than a service warning. Compliance load is much higher — no "cures acne", no "clinically proven" without a citation, and `#ad` is mandatory for creator content.

## 3. Cafe / restaurant — SENSORY & LOCAL

**Brand:** Filter & Froth Coffee House, Siripuram, Visakhapatnam · ₹120 filter coffee, ₹180 cold brew
**Top hooks:**
- *"This coffee was roasted nine days ago. I checked the bag."*
- *"Twenty-six seats. No laptops after 4 PM. Finally."*
- *"I came for the wifi. I stayed for the chai."*
**Script (Storytime, 25s):** "I came here to work and ended up talking to three strangers. Twenty-six seats, one grinder, and they date-stamp every bag — nine days old, maximum. The filter coffee is ₹120 and honestly better than the place near my office that charges ₹250. Fair warning: it gets loud after 6, and there's no wifi on weekends. That's kind of the point."
**Proof type:** roast dates, seat count, supplier names (Chikmagalur estate), regulars
**CTA:** "Walk in — they hold tables till 6:15" (micro) → "See what's brewing today" (mid)
**What changed:** the honest limitation flipped from a service warning to a *deliberate anti-feature* ("no wifi on weekends") — which is a positioning choice, not an apology. Urgency is daily (the 8 AM brew rush) rather than seasonal.

## 4. Fitness / gym — STRAIGHT-TALK, NO HYPE

**Brand:** Iron Yard Gym, Gajuwaka, Visakhapatnam · ₹2,500/month
**Top hooks:**
- *"I cancelled my ₹4,000 gym membership for this one."*
- *"Nobody here will talk to you about your 'journey'."*
- *"Forty people max per slot. That's the whole pitch."*
**Script (Objection-crusher, 28s):** "The reason most people quit a gym isn't motivation. It's waiting twenty minutes for a bench press. This place caps each slot at forty people and posts the number on the door. ₹2,500 a month, no joining fee, no yearly lock-in — cancel whenever. Honest part: it's not air-conditioned in summer, and mornings between 7 and 9 are packed. But the trainer knows your name by week one."
**Proof type:** capacity numbers, pricing transparency, named trainers, retention rate
**CTA:** "Come for one free session — no card needed" (micro) → "Book a free trial slot" (mid)
**What changed:** hooks attack the *category's* dishonesty rather than the product's weakness. Proof is operational (caps, timings, pricing). The tone has zero motivation-speak — in fitness, hype is the biggest authenticity killer.

## 5. SaaS tool — PROBLEM-FIRST, NO JARGON

**Brand:** BillKaro *(representative)* — GST invoicing app for Indian freelancers, ₹399/month
**Top hooks:**
- *"I spent four hours on one GST invoice. Never again."*
- *"My CA asked which software I use. That's when I knew."*
- *"Three minutes per invoice. I timed it."*
**Script (PAS, 25s):** "Freelancers: how many hours did you lose on invoicing last month? For me it was four hours on one invoice, because the GST format changed and my old template broke. This does it in about three minutes — GST number, the right rate, the format your client's CA won't argue with. Not for you if you have an accountant doing it end-to-end already; honestly, then you don't need this. ₹399 a month, first month free."
**Proof type:** a timed demo, a real screen recording, named integrations, a specific template
**CTA:** "Comment INVOICE and I'll send the free template" (micro) → "Start the free month — no card" (mid)
**What changed:** screens overtake faces (product-led UGC), hooks use *time wasted* as the universal currency, and the honest limitation is a genuine disqualifier ("if you have a CA, you don't need this") — which paradoxically converts better in SaaS than in any other category.

## 6. Dental clinic — WARM-CLINICAL, TRUST-FIRST

**Brand:** Sri Sai Dental, MVP Colony, Visakhapatnam · cleaning from ₹800
**Top hooks:**
- *"I hadn't been to a dentist in six years. I'll tell you what happened."*
- *"They showed me the bill before the treatment. Start to finish."*
- *"Ask them to show you the instrument before they open the packet."*
**Script (Founder-POV, 30s):** "Most people who come here haven't seen a dentist in years, and they're embarrassed about it. We don't say anything about that — we show you the X-ray, tell you what it costs, and let you decide. Cleaning starts at ₹800. If a tooth needs more work, you get it in writing before we start. One honest thing: we don't do braces on the spot. That's an orthodontist's call, and we'll refer you."
**Proof type:** qualifications, sterilisation process, visible pricing, referral honesty
**CTA:** "Walk in and ask for a look — the first assessment is free" (micro) → "Book a cleaning this week" (mid)
**What changed:** the "honest limitation" became a *referral* — publicly sending a patient elsewhere is the strongest trust signal in healthcare. Zero fear-based hooks, no pain claims, and every script needs a compliance pass (no treatment guarantees, no before/after implying outcomes for medical procedures).

## Side-by-side: what actually changes per brand type

| Dimension | Salon | D2C skincare | Cafe | Gym | SaaS | Dental |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook fuel | Customer stories | Skepticism | Sensory detail | Category bullshit | Hours wasted | Embarrassment |
| Proof unit | Faces, longevity | Ingredient list | Roast dates | Capacity numbers | Timed demo | Credentials + pricing |
| Honest limitation | Time in the chair | Not a quick fix | No wifi weekends | No AC in summer | You may not need it | We refer out |
| Urgency mechanism | Wedding season | Offer code | Daily brew rush | Slot caps | Free month | Appointment slots |
| CTA first step | Send a photo | Comment a word | Walk in | Free session | Free template | Free assessment |
| Compliance load | Medium | High | Low | Low–med | Medium | **Highest** |
| Never sounds like | Fake-luxury | Miracle claims | Corporate chain | Motivation-speak | Tech jargon | Fear-mongering |

**What stays constant:** the 24-hook generation, the 6 frameworks, the 2.5 words/second rule, the honest-limitation requirement, the one-small-ask CTA, the platform cutdown structure, and the compliance gate. That's the system. The voice is the variable.
