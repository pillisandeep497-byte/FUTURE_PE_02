## POST 1 — the main submission post

> **I wrote 24 hooks for one salon. Here's what I learned about why most UGC ads fail.** 🎬

> Every local business is being told to "make UGC content". Most of them end up with a video that looks like a phone video but talks like a TV ad.
>
> For my Future Interns Prompt Engineering internship (Task 2), I built a **prompt system that generates UGC-style ad content** — hooks, scripts, CTAs, platform cutdowns and shot briefs.
>
> **The client:** a hair & bridal studio in MVP Colony, Visakhapatnam. 4 chairs, 7 years old, zero video content.
>
> **Three things I learned:**
>
> **1. The hook is 80% of the ad.** So I generate 24 of them — across 10 archetypes — then score each on scroll-stop, clarity, honesty and ad-speak. Only the top 6 get shot. The winning hook was: *"I spent ₹12,000 on keratin before I found this place."* Money on the table plus a confession beats any benefit statement.
>
> **2. Every script needs one honest weakness.** "You'll be in the chair three to four hours. Bring a charger." "We'll say no if your hair can't take it." "It's not the cheapest in MVP Colony." One admission makes the other 90% believable. Remove it and the whole ad reads like an ad.
>
> **3. UGC ads run as PAID media — so compliance is part of the writing.** No "guaranteed", no "permanent", no fake urgency, before/after needs "results vary", paid creator content needs #ad, and trending audio can't be used for paid ads. Most AI UGC systems ignore this completely.
>

> • 24 scored hooks across 10 archetypes
> • 6 scripts using 6 different frameworks (PAS, before/after, storytime, objection-crusher, review-reaction, founder-POV)
> • 18 CTAs on a friction ladder + 12 captions + pinned-comment strategy
> • Platform cutdowns: Reels 30/15/7s, Shorts, Meta ad copy
> • A shot brief a stylist can film on a phone between clients, and a consent script
> • A 30-point QA scorecard — where I logged the 11 ad-clichés my own first draft contained

## POST 2 — the teaching post (2–3 days later, usually performs better)

> **I asked AI to write a UGC ad. It wrote a corporate voiceover. Here's the actual first line:**

> ❌ *"Transform your hair with our premium keratin treatment! Book now!"*
>
> Seven words, four crimes. "Transform". "Premium". "Book now". And an exclamation mark.
>
> If you've tried generating UGC scripts with AI, you've probably got something like this. Here's why it happens and the three fixes that worked.
>
> **1. AI writes the average of the internet — and the average ad is bad.**
> Fix: give the model a banned list. Mine bans 25+ phrases ("game-changer", "must-have", "say goodbye to", "premium quality"). Once "transform" is off the table, the model has to find something specific. That's when *"I spent ₹12,000 before I found this place"* shows up.
>
> **2. Hooks need volume, not cleverness.**
> Fix: generate 24 hooks across 10 archetypes — problem call-out, confession, contrarian, POV, objection, social proof, local, before/after, founder, urgency. Then score each. Cleverness is luck; volume is a process.
>
> **3. Real UGC says something the brand wouldn't.**
> Fix: make one honest limitation mandatory in every script. *"Fair warning — three to four hours in the chair. Bring a charger."* That line did more for believability than any benefit I could have written.
>
> Bonus fix that nobody talks about: UGC ads are **paid media**. If your AI wrote "guaranteed results" or a before/after without "results vary", the ad gets rejected — or the account gets restricted.

## POST 3 — the "product" post (post when you want clients)

> **What I'd write for your business if you gave me 30 minutes.**

> I've been building a UGC ad system for local businesses. Here's the process, using a Visakhapatnam salon as the example.
>
> **Step 1 — Find the number your customer already feels.** Not "great service". Numbers: "40 minutes with a dryer every morning", "240 hours a year". Recognition beats persuasion.
>
> **Step 2 — Ask the owner what she refuses to do.** The salon owner turns down ₹6,000 bookings when the hair can't take a treatment. That became the best ad in the pack, because no competitor can say it — they'd have to actually do it.
>
> **Step 3 — Write five versions of the first 2 seconds.** The hook is the ad. Everything after it is just closing.
>
> **Step 4 — End with one small ask.** Not "book now". "Send a photo on WhatsApp — price in 10 minutes." Nobody books a 3-hour service from a 20-second video. Anybody will send a photo.
>
> **Step 5 — Hand over a shot list, not a script.** Most people get a script and film nothing. A shot list with timings, phone settings and a consent line gets filmed the same day.
>
