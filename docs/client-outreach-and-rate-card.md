# Client Outreach & Rate Card — turning Task 2 into paid work

The task says this can lead directly to paid work. It can — **if you lead with finished content instead of a proposal.** Nobody buys "I can write UGC scripts". Everybody wants to see three hooks written for *their* business.

---

## The 20-minute winning move: pre-write before you pitch

Before you message any business, spend 20 minutes writing **3 hooks + 1 mini-script** for them specifically. From their Google reviews, their Instagram and one visit.

Then your first message isn't a pitch. It's a delivery.

**WhatsApp / DM script:**
> Hi [name], I'm Sandeep — I write UGC-style ad scripts, mainly for businesses in Vizag. I had a look at your page and wrote three hooks for you, free, no strings. Here's one:
>
> *"I spent ₹12,000 on two keratins before I found this place."* (that one's for the keratin service)
>
> Want me to send the other two? If they're useful, use them. If not, delete them — genuinely no problem.

**Why this converts:** you're offering a finished thing, not asking for a meeting. The reply rate on this approach is roughly 5–10× a standard "hi sir I do digital marketing" message.

## The offer ladder

| Tier | Deliverable | Price | Their risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Free sample** | 3 hooks + 1 script for their business | ₹0 | None |
| **1. Starter** | 10 hooks + 2 scripts + CTAs + captions | ₹2,000 | Low |
| **2. Standard** ⭐ | 15 hooks + 4 scripts + CTAs + captions + shot brief + WhatsApp reply templates | ₹5,000 | Low |
| **3. Campaign** | Everything in Standard + 3 platform cutdowns + Meta ad copy + 2 revision rounds + 7-day edit support | ₹10,000 | Medium |
| **4. Retainer** | 4 new scripts + 8 hooks + captions monthly | ₹6,000–₹8,000/month | Medium |

**Market context:** Indian agencies pay roughly **₹2,000–₹6,000 per UGC script**. A script + shot brief + edit guidance bundle routinely sells for ₹5,000–₹15,000. Freelance UGC *creators* (who film too) charge ₹8,000–₹25,000 per video — you're selling the script layer above that.

**Your pricing logic (say this out loud in the pitch):**
> "One extra booking covers this several times over. And you keep the scripts forever — they work on Reels, WhatsApp status and as Meta ads."

## Who to pitch (and who to skip)

**Good targets — local businesses that are already spending but have no content:**
- Salons, spas, bridal studios (before/after content is native)
- Dental clinics, physiotherapy, dermatology (trust-led, high ticket)
- Gyms and fitness studios (before/after + testimonials)
- Cafes and restaurants (sensory b-roll, easy to shoot)
- Boutiques and jewellery shops (bridal season)
- Coaching institutes (results-driven)

**Also strong — D2C brands already running ads:** search Meta Ad Library for brands in your city. If they're spending on paid ads, they need fresh creative every month. That's a retainer conversation.

**Skip for now:** businesses with no Instagram at all (they won't see the value) and large chains (procurement cycles, no decision-maker access).

## Pitch deck in 5 lines (verbal, not a file)

1. "Your competitors post before-after photos. Everyone scrolls past those."
2. "I write ads that open with what your customer is already feeling — like 'If your hair takes 40 minutes to dry, this is for you.'"
3. "Here are 24 hooks I wrote for a salon in MVP Colony, and 6 scripts." *(show the pack)*
4. "Everything is shootable on your phone in an hour. I give your staff a shot list — who stands where, what light, what to say."
5. "Standard pack is ₹5,000. Want me to write the first three hooks for your business today, free?"

## Objection handling

**"We already have someone doing our Instagram."**
> "Then this makes their job easier — I'll write the scripts, they shoot them. Most content people hate writing scripts anyway."

**"₹5,000 is a lot."**
> "One bridal booking is ₹18,000. This is 4 scripts, 15 hooks and a shot brief — the content for a whole month. If the first script gets you one client, it's paid for."

**"Will this actually get views?"**
> "Scripts don't guarantee views — the algorithm decides. What I guarantee is that the first two seconds are engineered to stop the scroll, that the sales message is clear, and that nothing in it breaks ad policy. That's the part almost everyone gets wrong."

**"Can you just film it too?"**
> "I can direct it and give you the shot list and consent forms, and you or your staff film it — that keeps the authenticity, which is the whole point. If you want a creator to film, I'll brief them too."

**"Send me a proposal."**
> Don't. Send the **free 3 hooks** instead. A proposal is a document they'll ignore; a hook is something they'll read.

## Delivery workflow (so you don't overpromise)

| Day | What you do |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Brief call (30 min) using `prompts/00` |
| Day 1–2 | 24 hooks → score → promote 6 |
| Day 2–3 | 6 scripts, one per framework; read aloud; cut |
| Day 3 | CTAs, captions, pinned comments, DM replies |
| Day 4 | Shot brief + consent script + edit handoff |
| Day 5 | Compliance scan (`prompts/05`), fix flags, deliver `content-pack.md` |

**Total: ~5 hours of work for tier 2.** That's ₹1,000/hour at ₹5,000 — and it gets faster every time you run the system.

---

## After delivery (this is where repeat clients come from)

1. **Ask for one thing:** "Send me a 30-second clip of the response when you post it."
2. **Offer the edit:** "Send me your raw footage, I'll cut the 15s and 7s versions for ₹1,500." (Low effort, high perceived value.)
3. **Set the next month:** "Want me to write 4 more scripts for December, and the bridal season angle?"
4. **Ask for a referral:** "Do you know one other business owner in the area who'd want this?" — referrals from a local business owner are the warmest leads you'll ever get.
